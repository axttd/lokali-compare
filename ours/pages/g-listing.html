The story

As the sun sets, the river goes quiet. We paddle into the mangroves with no engines and no spotlights. Then the berembang trees begin to blink, thousands of fireflies flashing together in perfect time.

Colonies like this are rare, fewer than 20 remain worldwide, and paddling keeps them undisturbed. It's gentle enough for children and first-timers, and couples love the stillness.

Most guests go silent when the first tree lights up, and talk about it all the way back home.

Paddle-only, no lights, small groups

Engines and torches stress the fireflies, so we paddle and keep groups to 10. Part of every booking buys mangrove seedlings — the village has replanted 4,200 since 2024.

Good to know

  • What to bring11

    Long sleeves and trousers keep mosquitoes off. Wear sandals or old trainers that can get wet, and bring a refillable water bottle and a light rain jacket.

  • Etiquette12

    On the water, keep voices low and use no flash or torches, as light stops the fireflies blinking. Please don't touch the trees. In the village, cover shoulders and knees, and take shoes off indoors.

  • Food and dietary13

    Contains peanuts and coconut · add allergies at booking

    Teh tarik and kuih are served at Pak Rahim's home, from a halal kitchen. Vegetarian on request.

  • Accessibility14

    Not wheelchair accessible, as boarding needs a step down into the boat. It's a 300 m walk on a flat wooden boardwalk. Toilets are at the jetty and the host's home, and boat seats have back support.

FAQs20

What if it rains?

Light rain is fine. In heavy rain the host moves you to another date or you get a full refund.

Is it safe for young children?

Yes, from age 5. Child life jackets are provided and boats stay close to the bank.

Can I take photos of the fireflies?

Without flash only. Most phones can't capture them — the host shares a long-exposure photo after the trip.
